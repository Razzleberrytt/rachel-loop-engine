# Retention Rules

These are heuristics, not laws. Rachel-specific experiment data can override them.

## Opening

The first second should immediately provide at least one of:
- a meaningful visual event
- a reaction
- a surprising statement
- a clear unresolved question
- a payoff glimpse
- an emotionally recognizable situation

Avoid:
- camera settling
- throat-clearing
- "so today we're going to..." unless the wording itself is the hook
- context that can be inferred later

## Information density

Every segment should perform at least one job:
- advance the story
- increase curiosity
- deliver emotion
- clarify necessary context
- create anticipation
- deliver payoff
- bridge the loop

If it performs none, cut or compress it.

## Pattern changes

When a clip is visually static for too long, consider a natural change:
- punch-in
- crop shift
- reaction cut
- B-roll already present in source
- caption emphasis

Do not manufacture changes every fixed number of seconds.

## Payoff timing

Do not automatically delay the payoff. When possible, give the audience an early taste of the payoff and let the story explain it.

## Runtime

Shorter is not always better. Optimize for **necessary runtime**: the shortest version that preserves comprehension, authenticity, emotional rhythm, and payoff.

## Ending

The ending should be intentional. Choose one:
- satisfying stop
- emotional button
- open loop
- seamless restart
- visual match
- sentence continuation

An accidental empty tail is never an acceptable ending.

## Anti-rules

Do not blindly apply:
- a cut every 1–2 seconds
- huge captions
- constant zooms
- artificial controversy
- fake surprise
- forced cliffhangers
- misleading omission
