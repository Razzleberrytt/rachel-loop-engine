# Rachel Editing Style

This is the evolving Rachel-specific style layer. Evidence from Rachel's own videos outranks generic social-media conventions.

## Current baseline

- Preserve candid, family-centered authenticity.
- The video should feel like a real moment that happened to be edited well, not an advertisement pretending to be a real moment.
- Protect spontaneous reactions; they are often the content.
- Favor invisible editing over effects that announce themselves.
- Loops should feel accidental or inevitable, not like a gimmick.
- Avoid an obvious verbal sign-off if it weakens replay behavior.
- Avoid stuffing the final second with "follow for more" unless there is a separate strategic reason.

## Pacing

- Cut hesitation when it is merely dead time.
- Keep hesitation when it is part of a reaction, joke, tension, or personality.
- Do not accelerate every sentence into unnatural speech.
- Do not remove every breath.

## Captions

- Clean and readable.
- Prefer sentence/phrase chunks over hyperactive karaoke captions.
- Use emphasis selectively.
- Keep captions away from the lower UI danger zone.
- Preserve the audience's ability to watch the people rather than the text.

## Visuals

- Favor close attention to faces/reactions without permanently over-cropping.
- Use movement cuts and punch-ins to hide structural edits.
- Preserve enough environmental context to understand family moments.
- No beauty filter or face modification by default.

## Humor / cute moments

Do not explain the joke before it lands. If the raw footage contains a naturally funny/cute reveal, structure around the reveal rather than narrating over it.

## Loop preference

Rachel's looping videos are a strategic area of interest. Treat loop design as a first-class edit decision, but never sacrifice comprehensibility solely to manufacture replays.

## Provisional hypothesis from recent content

A restart that is difficult to perceive may increase repeat viewing when the closing cadence does not announce that the video is over. This remains a hypothesis to validate with multiple posts, not a permanent rule yet.
